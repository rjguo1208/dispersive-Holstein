Project plan
An isolated defect in the dispersive Holstein model

A staged plan from analytic limits to the quantum lattice: what each step does, which method it uses, what it produces and when it counts as done.

Plan, 2026-09-23; implementation has not started. For the model and the physics see the Theory notes. Scope: one dimension, one electron, zero temperature; only the ground states and static response for N=0N=0N=0 and N=1N=1N=1. No defect excited states (no Huang–Rhys model) and no real materials.

1. Goals and questions

Goal: in the dispersive Holstein defect model of the theory notes, answer quantitatively how a defect changes the local electronic states, the local phonon states and the electron–phonon coupling, and how the three induce one another. Four specific questions:

  1. Direct and induced. Does the table in Section 4 of the theory notes hold for the quantum lattice? How large is each induced effect?
  2. Share of the local mode. What fraction of the static relaxation energy of the bound electron does the local vibrational mode carry (flocf_{\mathrm{loc}}floc​)? How does it vary with ξe/ξph\xi_e/\xi_{\mathrm{ph}}ξe​/ξph​?
  3. Electron-induced softening and the isotope effect. Are they largest in the intermediate regime Ebind∼ωlocE_{\mathrm{bind}}\sim\omega_{\mathrm{loc}}Ebind​∼ωloc​?
  4. Role of dispersion. As κ=K/K0\kappa=K/K_0κ=K/K0​ grows, how do the binding energy, the range of the distortion and the trapping threshold for coupling only at the defect change?

Outside this plan: defect excited states and spectra, scattering states and the T-matrix, finite temperature, finite electron density, bond (Peierls) coupling and real materials.

2.2 Energy zero

All energies are measured from the N=0N=0N=0 ground state of the same lattice, so the zero-point energy cancels:

ΔE=EN=1−EN=0,Ebind=Epol(k=0)−ΔE.\Delta E=E_{N=1}-E_{N=0},\qquad E_{\mathrm{bind}}=E_{\mathrm{pol}}(k=0)-\Delta E .ΔE=EN=1​−EN=0​,Ebind​=Epol​(k=0)−ΔE.

Epol(k=0)E_{\mathrm{pol}}(k=0)Epol​(k=0) is the polaron band bottom of the clean infinite chain, computed with k-space VED. The ΔE\Delta EΔE of a bound state converges exponentially with chain length, whereas a band bottom computed on a finite open chain carries a 1/L21/L^21/L2 error, so the two are computed separately.

Local-oscillator representation, used for ED and DMRG:

zjz_jzj​ is the number of neighbour springs attached to site jjj: 2 inside an open chain and 1 at its ends. The neighbour term contains bjbj+1b_jb_{j+1}bj​bj+1​ and bj†bj+1†b_j^\dagger b_{j+1}^\daggerbj†​bj+1†​; all terms are kept, with no rotating-wave approximation. The aaa vacuum is the N=0N=0N=0 ground state. The phonon part conserves particle number and only the coupling creates phonons, so a Bonča–Trugman-type variational basis needs to cover only the neighbourhood of the electron. The matrix elements of D1/2D^{1/2}D1/2 and D−1/4D^{-1/4}D−1/4 decay exponentially with distance. 4. Content and acceptance criteria of each stage

Stage 0: shared code and tests

  • Content: One module defines the parameters (t, Mj, K0,j, K, αj, εd, Lt,\ M_j,\ K_{0,j},\ K,\ \alpha_j,\ \varepsilon_d,\ Lt, Mj​, K0,j​, K, αj​, εd​, L), the force-constant matrix Φ\PhiΦ, the matrices D1/2D^{1/2}D1/2 and D−1/4D^{-1/4}D−1/4 needed by the two representations, and functions for the observables of Section 7 of the theory notes. All later stages call it.
  • Acceptance: Φ\PhiΦ is positive definite; for K→0K\to0K→0 the two representations agree term by term; the tests are committed with the code.

Stage 1: uncoupled limit

  • Content: Take α=αd=0\alpha=\alpha_d=0α=αd​=0. The electronic side is the Koster–Slater bound state [1]; the phonon side is the local vibrational mode of a mass or stiffness defect [2].
  • Method: Direct diagonalization on a long open chain (L=2001L=2001L=2001), compared with the one-dimensional analytic Green's functions.
  • Outputs: EB, ξe, ⟨n0⟩E_B,\ \xi_e,\ \langle n_0\rangleEB​, ξe​, ⟨n0​⟩; ωloc, ξph, ρ00ph(ω)\omega_{\mathrm{loc}},\ \xi_{\mathrm{ph}},\ \rho^{\mathrm{ph}}_{00}(\omega)ωloc​, ξph​, ρ00ph​(ω); ⟨δu02⟩\langle\delta u_0^2\rangle⟨δu02​⟩ and ⟨δp02⟩\langle\delta p_0^2\rangle⟨δp02​⟩ at the defect in the N=0N=0N=0 ground state.
  • Acceptance: Numerical and analytic results agree to better than 10−1010^{-10}10−10 (checklist item V1).

Stage 2: adiabatic (classical-lattice) limit

  • Content: For a given uuu, find the electronic ground state, update u=−Φ−1Fu=-\Phi^{-1}Fu=−Φ−1F with F=α∘ρF=\alpha\circ\rhoF=α∘ρ, and iterate to self-consistency, starting from both localized and extended initial states to detect multiple solutions. Stage 5: dispersion and dimension

  • Content: Compare κ=0, 0.1, 0.25\kappa=0,\ 0.1,\ 0.25κ=0, 0.1, 0.25; study how the threshold for coupling only at the defect changes with ω0/t\omega_0/tω0​/t and κ\kappaκ. Optionally, use a Bethe lattice or two dimensions to look at thresholds and bistability in higher dimensions.

  • Acceptance: Energy errors and deviations of observables are reported as explicit numbers; NNQS alone is not used to draw physical conclusions.

9. Decision points

At each decision point the results and options are posted on the website, and work continues after you confirm.

  1. D1 (after stage 2): choose the focus region for the quantum calculations (the parameter range of the intermediate regime) and the values of κ\kappaκ.
  2. D2 (after stage 3): choose the production method (DMRG or VED) and the convergence criteria.
  3. D3 (after stage 4): decide the direction of extension: dispersion and dimension, NNQS, bond coupling, or spin and charge states.
